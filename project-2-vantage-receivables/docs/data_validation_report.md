# Data Validation Report
## Vantage Wholesale Supply — Receivables Performance

**Reporting date:** 2025-12-31  
**Dataset:** 29,971 invoices · 27,601 cash receipts · 29,312 cash applications · 1,425 adjustments · 1,347 promises · 400 customers  
**Status:** validated — 25/25 acceptance tests pass, 27/27 SQL-versus-Excel checks reconcile

> **Data disclosure.** Vantage Wholesale Supply is a fictional company. Every figure in this
> project is synthetic and generated by the scripts in `sql/`. No confidential data is used and
> no claim is made about any production system.

---

## 1. What "validated" means here

A dataset is not validated because a query returned rows. It is validated when the things that
would let it produce confident, plausible, wrong answers have each been tested and found not to
be happening.

| Mechanism | Catches | Blind to |
|---|---|---|
| **Constraints and creation gates** | Structurally impossible rows | Anything internally consistent but wrong |
| **Behavioural data-quality checks** (`sql/04`) | Records individually valid but collectively impossible | Defects that look like normal business behaviour |
| **Cross-implementation reconciliation** | A metric implemented twice that disagrees | A definition wrong in the same way twice |
| **Modelling adequacy** | A category the schema cannot express | Whatever nobody thought to model |

The fourth row was added after the fact described in §3.1, which is the most instructive finding
in this report.

## 3. Defects found during the build

### 3.1 A modelling limitation that was silently mis-attributing 2.38 days of DSO

This is not a coding defect. It is the more dangerous kind: a schema that could not express a
real category, so the category was charged to something else and nobody could tell.

The original model held a cash receipt and its application to an invoice in **one row**
(`Fact_Payment`). That makes two different questions collapse into one:

- *Is this invoice open?*
- *Does this customer owe us money?*

They are not the same question, and the difference between them is **unapplied cash** — money in
the bank that no remittance advice has matched. With a single table it had no representation at
all, so every dollar of it looked like a customer who had not paid.

**Splitting the fact into `Fact_CashReceipt` (the bank event) and `Fact_CashApplication`
(receipt → invoice) moved $419,392.13 — 2.38 days of DSO — out of "customers are paying late"
and into "we have not applied our own cash".**

It also made two further rules expressible:

- The date that reduces a balance is the **application** date, not the receipt date. Cash banked
  on 28 December and matched on 4 January must not cure a December ageing bucket. 36 applications
  worth $165,306 fall in exactly that window in this dataset.
- A receipt with no applications against it is fully unapplied. 57 receipts are in that state,
  the oldest for 679 days.

**The lesson:** a modelling shortcut that cannot represent a category will not leave a gap in the
output. It will attribute that category to whatever it *can* represent, and the result looks
complete.

### 3.2 Instalment plans stalled permanently on discount terms — fixed

The payment generator paid 60% of a partial invoice on the first instalment and only emitted the
second when the invoice was *not* a discount-taker:

```sql
-- the amount rule and the row filter disagreed about the same condition
* CASE WHEN i.IsPartial = 0 THEN 1.0 WHEN s.Seq = 1 THEN 0.6 ELSE 0.4 END
...
AND (s.Seq = 1 OR (i.IsPartial = 1 AND i.TakesDiscount = 0))
```

208 invoices carried a 40% residual that could never clear. Because `2/10NET30` is the only terms
code with a discount, the entire defect landed on one cohort and presented as a striking finding:

> *"47.4% of open `2/10NET30` invoices are more than 90 days past due, against 2.3% on NET30."*

That finding was false. Every row was internally consistent, the settlement identity held to the
cent, and the ageing buckets summed correctly. It surfaced only when open AR was cut by ageing
**and** payment terms together.

The fix is domain logic rather than a patch — a settlement discount is earned by paying in full
inside the discount window, so a discount-taker is never a partial payer:

```sql
UPDATE #Inv SET IsPartial = 0 WHERE TakesDiscount = 1;
```

**UAT-15** is the regression guard. Its premise had to be revised once unapplied cash existed: a
part-paid invoice can now legitimately stall because the receipt covering its remaining
instalment was never matched. The test therefore asserts that no stall is *unexplained* — one
remains, against a tolerance of five.

### 3.3 Write-off booked at face value over an existing credit memo — fixed

The generator wrote off the full invoice amount even where a credit memo had already reduced what
was owed, leaving five invoices with adjustments exceeding their own value. Found by
`ADJUSTMENT_EXCEEDS_INVOICE`; now 0.

### 3.4 Two published figures that disagreed with their own components — fixed

`AvgDaysDelinquent` is `DSO − BPDSO`. All three were rounded to two decimals independently, so
the page published 63.07, 46.27 and **16.81** while a reader doing the subtraction got **16.80**.
Caught by **UAT-11**.

Earlier in the build the same class appeared differently: `fn_DSO` divided before multiplying
while `fn_DSOBridge` multiplied before dividing, so the two objects published a classic DSO
differing by one cent. Caught by **UAT-05**.

Both are now governed by one rule, applied in SQL *and* in the Excel implementation: **a derived
figure is computed from its components as published, not from their unrounded intermediates.**

## 4. Defect deliberately retained

**96 duplicate cash receipts**, worth $371,039.99, producing 101 over-applied invoices.

These are planted and they stay. A data-quality layer nobody has watched fire is not a control,
it is an assertion. **UAT-17** asserts that exactly 96 duplicate bankings are found and that they
are the sole cause of over-application.

Note that `DUPLICATE_RECEIPT` and `OVER_APPLIED_CASH` report the *same* $371,039.99 — the cause
and its effect are the same money, which is precisely why only one of them counts toward
exposure.

**Gate result: FAIL.** AR is mis-stated by $371,039.99, which is **3.421%** of the $10,847,081.49
open balance, against a 1.000% tolerance. The gate fails on purpose and the build does not
suppress it.

### Two design points in this table

**`CountsTowardExposure` stops double counting.** An earlier version summed `AmountAtRisk` across
every check and reported $954k of mis-statement against a true $381k. Each defect now carries an
`ImpactClass`, and only the check that actually mis-states the balance contributes to the total.
The threshold is a percentage of open AR rather than a fixed dollar figure, because an absolute
threshold silently tightens or loosens as the book grows.

**`EntityType` is declared in the catalog, not inferred from the rows a check returns.** A check
that finds nothing has no rows to infer from, so it was falling back to the invoice count and
reporting its rate against the wrong denominator — correct only while it was failing. Both
zero-count checks above now scan the right population (29,312 applications, not 29,971 invoices).

The countback DSO agreement is the one worth pausing on: SQL computes it with a windowed
running-sum and a `TOP 1`; Excel computes it with a twelve-row helper block, `AGGREGATE`, `MATCH`
and `INDEX`. Two genuinely different implementations of a non-trivial algorithm agreeing to one
thousandth of a day is meaningful evidence that the definition is right.

### Residual differences, and one that was the test's fault

Differences are not all exactly zero and should not be: SQL rounds to `DECIMAL(9,2)` at
publication while Excel carries full binary precision, so half-a-cent is the correct tolerance.

Two checks originally failed for reasons that were the *test's*, not the data's:

1. A check compared Excel's twelve-month sales total against SQL's `SalesPerDay` multiplied back
   up by 365, magnifying a half-cent rounding into a $1.02 gap. It now compares per-day.
2. The promise kept-rate summed the whole extract in Excel while SQL windowed to twelve months, a
   0.02-point definitional mismatch. The Excel formula now carries the same window, so the two
   are comparing the same population.

## 7. Structural assertions

verified by `sql/08_uat_test_cases.sql`, **25 of 25 passing**.

| Property | Test | Why it would matter if it broke |
|---|---|---|
| Settlement identity holds for every invoice | UAT-01 | One definition underpins every downstream figure |
| The as-of balance counts only cash applied by then, to invoices raised by then | UAT-02 | Otherwise every past ageing report improves over time |
| Every open invoice falls in exactly one ageing bucket | UAT-03 | Overlaps double-count; gaps drop dollars silently |
| DSO bridge components sum to classic DSO exactly | UAT-04 | A residual invites plugging it into the nearest component |
| The same metric in two objects agrees | UAT-05 | Two published figures that can disagree eventually will |
| Shift-share closes exactly | UAT-06 | Otherwise the rate/mix split is not evidence of anything |
| Customer rollup ties to invoice grain | UAT-07 | A fan-out join invents or loses money |
| Collectable exposure is bounded and non-negative | UAT-08 | Negative exposure sorts the worst accounts to the bottom |
| Every queued account has exactly one known action | UAT-09 | A row with no instruction is not a control |
| No collector exceeds capacity without an escalation | UAT-10 | An unbounded worklist is a ledger with a sort order |
| ADD uses one method on both sides, and matches its own components | UAT-11 | Mixing methods yields a number with no interpretation |
| Over-applied cash is surfaced, not netted | UAT-12 | Netting understates total AR and hides the defect |
| Paper collections gap cannot be negative | UAT-13 | Would mean one CEI variant is reading the wrong rows |
| Procedures reject invalid arguments | UAT-14 | A silent fallback answers a question nobody asked |
| No part-paid invoice stalls without cash behind it | UAT-15 | Regression guard on §3.2 |
| Generator is deterministic | UAT-16 | Quoted figures must be re-checkable |
| Planted defects are still detected | UAT-17 | An untested detector is an assertion, not a control |
| **Unapplied cash is floored per receipt** | **UAT-18** | Netting an over-applied receipt against genuine backlog hides accounts about to be wrongly chased |
| **NetExposure = OpenBalance − UnappliedCash** | **UAT-19** | The figure a collector quotes on the call |
| **No `APPLY_CASH` account reaches a worklist** | **UAT-20** | Otherwise the queue causes the exact harm it exists to prevent |
| **Cash cycle = billing lag + classic DSO** | **UAT-21** | Lag is additive to DSO, not part of it; folding it in breaks UAT-04 |
| **Promise status is derived, never stored** | **UAT-22** | A stored outcome would be self-reported by the party measured |
| **An unripe promise is Outstanding, not Broken** | **UAT-23** | Otherwise the kept-rate depends on when you ran the report |
| **No application precedes its receipt** | **UAT-24** | Cures an ageing bucket before the money arrived |
| **At-risk days-beyond-terms is never flattered by survivorship** | **UAT-25** | Settled DBT alone lets a failing cohort read as improving, because unpaid invoices stop being averaged |

## 8. Known limitations

1. **Promise-to-pay is modelled at account level, not invoice level.** A promise is made for an
   amount and settled by whatever remittance arrives, which matches how collections actually
   works, but it means a promise cannot be tied to the specific invoices it covers.

2. **Unapplied cash is aged by receipt date only.** A partially-applied receipt carries one age
   for its whole remainder, where a real cash-application team would track each open item.

3. **Billing lag has no sub-regional attribution.** The Southeast finding is at regional level;
   isolating it to a specific distribution centre or posting run would need a site dimension.

4. **The promise register has no contact channel or collector notes**, so kept-rate cannot be
   analysed by how the promise was obtained.

5. **The first four months are not comparable.** The ledger opens on 2024-01-01 and no balance
   can reach the 90+ bucket before late April 2024. Those months are published but flagged
   `IsComparablePeriod = 0`, and every trend statement is restricted to the comparable window.

6. **Portfolio aggregates hide cohort failures**, by construction rather than by defect. Three
   metrics read Green at portfolio level while a cohort inside them has failed. This is
   documented in the case study rather than "fixed", because the aggregate is not wrong — it is
   simply not sufficient, and each of the three now ships with its cohort cut attached.
