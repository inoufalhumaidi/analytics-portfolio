# Project 2 — Vantage Wholesale Supply: Receivables Performance

**Of the days DSO has risen, how many did Vantage grant itself through the terms it sold, how many
are customers taking beyond those terms — and given that split, who should each collector call
first?**

*Stack: T-SQL (SQL Server) · Power Query · Excel. No Power BI in this project — the deliverable is
a workbook the collections team opens every morning.*

> **Data disclosure.** Vantage Wholesale Supply is a fictional industrial parts distributor. All
> data is synthetic and generated by the scripts in `sql/`. No confidential data is used and no
> claim is made about any production system.

---

## The answer

DSO rose from **55.85 to 63.07 days** over the comparable window. The rise splits:

| | Days |
|---|---:|
| **Granted** — longer terms Vantage sold | **+1.94** |
| **Taken** — customers paying later | **+5.28** |
| **Total** | **+7.22** |

A shift-share decomposition shows the granted quarter was deliberate: weighted average terms rose
2.28 days, of which **+1.71 is rate effect** — the same 400 customers re-papered onto longer
terms, not a change in who was buying.

**But the two-way split was incomplete.** Of the 16.80 days currently sitting in "taken",
**2.38 days are Vantage's own cash**: $419,392 banked against customer accounts that nobody has
matched to an invoice. It is not lateness at all. True customer lateness is 14.42 days.

And the target is unreachable regardless:

> **The 45-day DSO target is arithmetically unachievable.** Weighted average terms sold are
> **46.68 days**. If every customer paid exactly on the due date, DSO would settle near 46.7 and
> the target would still be missed.

## The model decision that changed a conclusion

`Fact_CashReceipt` (the bank event) and `Fact_CashApplication` (receipt → invoice) are separate
facts. That is not tidiness — it is the difference between two questions:

- *Is this invoice open?*
- *Does this customer owe us money?*

Holding a receipt and its application in one row makes those the same question, so **unapplied
cash has no representation** and every dollar of it looks like a customer who has not paid.
Splitting them moved 2.38 days of DSO out of customer behaviour and into Vantage's own cash
application backlog.

It also makes the rule enforceable that the **application** date reduces a balance, never the
receipt date — so cash banked on 28 December and matched on 4 January cannot cure a December
ageing bucket. 36 applications worth $165,306 fall in exactly that window here.

## The Excel workbook

`excel/Vantage_AR_Control.xlsx` — **a control, not a report.**

Nothing on the Dashboard is a pasted number. Balances come from `SUMIFS` over the open-item
extract, countback DSO from a real month-by-month exhaustion loop on the Calc sheet, and the
red/amber/green status from `INDEX`/`MATCH` against the target table.

| Sheet | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Dashboard** | Scorecard, DSO bridge, true cash cycle, ageing profile — all recomputed by formula |
| **Priority Queue** | Who to call, in what order, with the instruction and the dollar ask |
| **Cash Application** | The *other* worklist: money already banked that nobody matched. These accounts must not be called |
| **Ageing Matrix** | Open AR by bucket, cut by any dimension, via `SUMIFS` with an `INDEX`/`MATCH` criteria column |
| **Validation** | Every headline figure computed twice — SQL and Excel — and reconciled |
| **Control** | Every parameter the workbook obeys |
| **Calc** | The working behind countback DSO and the queue ranking. Visible on purpose |

**No dynamic-array formulas.** `FILTER`, `SORT` and `UNIQUE` would shorten several formulas and
break the file on Excel 2019 and LibreOffice.

All **27** reconciliation checks pass. The one worth pausing on: SQL computes countback DSO with a
windowed running sum and a `TOP 1`; Excel computes it with a twelve-row helper block, `AGGREGATE`,
`MATCH` and `INDEX`. They agree to **0.0011 days**.

## The priority action queue

272 accounts carry a past-due balance. The queue ranks on **collectable exposure** — ageing-weighted
past-due dollars, less 75% of the ageing-weighted *disputed* dollars, less cash already banked
against the account — then bounds itself to what the team can work:

**65 accounts, 10–12 per collector, carrying 81.3% of the collectable exposure.**

| Action | Accounts | Past due | Exposure | On today's list |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `ESCALATE` | 19 | $758,532 | $359,632 | 19 |
| `COLLECTION_CALL` | 26 | $476,443 | $155,712 | 15 |
| `STANDARD_DUNNING` | 100 | $634,679 | $144,147 | 21 |
| `FINAL_NOTICE` | 19 | $356,211 | $80,901 | 5 |
| `RESOLVE_DISPUTE` | 45 | $195,694 | $36,407 | 5 |
| `COURTESY_REMINDER` | 12 | $26,036 | $6,509 | 0 |
| **`APPLY_CASH`** | **51** | **$303,692** | **$5,011** | **0** |

`APPLY_CASH` outranks everything. 51 accounts have cash already banked covering at least half
their past-due balance; ringing a customer who has already paid is the most damaging call a
collections team can make. **UAT-20 asserts none of them can reach a collector's worklist.**

## Data quality

The gate **fails**, and the build does not suppress it:

> `QA GATE: FAIL — AR mis-stated by 3.421%, above the 1.000% tolerance.`

96 duplicate cash receipts mis-state receivables by **$371,039.99**. They are planted and
retained: a data-quality layer nobody has watched fire is an assertion, not a control.

`DUPLICATE_RECEIPT` and `OVER_APPLIED_CASH` report the *same* $371,039.99 — cause and effect are
the same money, which is why only the effect counts toward exposure.

## Defects worth reading about

**A false finding that passed every balance check.** The generator paid 60% of a partial invoice
and only emitted the second instalment for non-discount invoices, so 208 invoices kept a 40%
residual that could never clear. Since `2/10NET30` is the only terms code with a discount, it
presented as a clean insight — *"47.4% of discount-terms invoices are 90+ days past due"* — that
was entirely false. Only a cut of **ageing against terms** exposed it.

**A workbook that reported success while broken.** The build printed "Saved"; the file was full of
`#REF!`, because Excel does not defer a reference to a sheet that does not exist yet. Worse, a
layout collision made Best Possible DSO read a *plausible* 42.75 instead of 46.19.

**A test whose premise my own feature invalidated.** UAT-02 subtracted all cash applied after 30
June from the December figure. Once applications could legitimately fall *after* the reporting
date, that removed $165,415 which had never been in the December total. The test failed; the data
was right.

Full write-ups in [docs/data_validation_report.md](docs/data_validation_report.md).

```bash
sqlcmd -S <server> -E -d VantageAR -i sql/07_stored_procedures.sql
sqlcmd -S <server> -E -d VantageAR -i sql/08_uat_test_cases.sql     # expect 25/25

powershell erp_extracts/Export_Extracts.ps1
powershell excel/Build_Workbook.ps1
powershell excel/Validate_Workbook.ps1                              # expect 27/27
```

The generator is deterministic — hash-keyed rather than `RAND()` — so a rebuild reproduces the
dataset byte for byte, and UAT-16 asserts all five fingerprints. Every figure quoted here is
re-checkable.

To browse the workbook without SQL Server, open `excel/Vantage_AR_Control.xlsx` directly: it ships
with its data loaded.
