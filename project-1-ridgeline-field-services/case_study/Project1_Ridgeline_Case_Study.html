01 · Executive summary

Ridgeline Field Services dispatches HVAC and electrical technicians across five regions. Leadership's standing question was simple to ask and, until this project, expensive to answer with a spreadsheet: where is technician capacity sitting idle, and where is it overcommitted — and what, specifically, should change?

Three findings drive the recommendations in this report:

  1. South Metro's utilization never once overlaps with any other region's across the full two-year window (41.1%–55.3%, vs. 59.3%+ everywhere else) — a structural staffing/dispatch imbalance, not a one-off dip.
  2. East Valley's SLA problem is invisible in the aggregate number (97.9% overall) and only appears when urgent, 1-hour-window jobs are isolated (93.2%) — a travel-distance-driven gap that a single blended KPI would hide.
  3. Apprentice technicians run a callback rate roughly 4.7x higher than Master technicians (21.6% vs 4.6%), concentrated enough to show up repeatedly in the priority action queue below.

All three are backed by a reusable SQL layer, cross-validated against an independently-built Excel formula layer — a cross-check that caught two real bugs during this project's build (Section 08), which is the actual argument for building the same logic twice rather than once.

02 · Business question & scope

Business question: Where is technician capacity being wasted, and which dispatch decisions should change this week?

Scope: five regions (North Metro, South Metro, East Valley, West Hills, Central District), ten HVAC/Electrical service types, 40 technicians (35 active, 5 inactive in this generation run), 300 clients, and 37,404 dispatched jobs across a 24-month window (Jan 2024–Dec 2025). Out of scope: real-time dispatch routing/GPS optimization, and multi-year demand forecasting — this is a monthly-refresh analytical model built to support a weekly dispatch-review cadence, not a live routing engine.

03 · Methodology & data validation

The analytical layer is a five-dimension/one-fact star schema in SQL Server (Dim_Date, Dim_Region, Dim_Technician, Dim_Client, Dim_ServiceType, Fact_ServiceJobs), plus a Ref_KPITargets table that centralizes every threshold used downstream — change a number there and the SQL views, the Excel dashboard, and the Power BI conditional formatting all move together.

The dataset is synthetic by design, not by necessity — a set-based T-SQL generator built in deliberate, documented imbalances (region workload intensity, travel-time baselines, skill-driven fix probabilities, HVAC/Electrical seasonality) so the KPI layer would have something real to find. It was then profiled — row counts, referential integrity, distribution sanity checks — before any dashboard was built on top of it. The full write-up, including a generation bug the profiling step caught (technicians dispatched before their hire date), is in docs/data_validation_report.md.

Every KPI is implemented as a parameterized SQL view/procedure (sql/04_kpi_views.sql, sql/05_stored_procedures.sql) and then re-implemented independently as live formulas in the Excel workbook — SUMIFS/AVERAGEIFS against a 37,404-row table, not a paste of query results. The two are cross-checked against each other rather than assumed to agree (Section 08).

This is not a one-month anomaly. Across all 24 months, South Metro's utilization ranges 41.1%–55.3% and never once overlaps with any other region's range (the next-lowest region, West Hills, never drops below 59.3%). South Metro is authorized for 8 technicians against a workload that consistently supports fewer. At the other end, East Valley averages the highest utilization of the five regions (79.2%), and several of its individual technicians post >95% utilization in a given month — an over-capacity/burnout risk (see the priority queue in Section 07).

05 · Finding 2 — Travel-driven SLA risk (a segment, not an aggregate)

SLA here is an arrival-window commitment (e.g. "technician arrives within 1 hour of the scheduled window"), not a multi-day completion deadline — this is how most residential/commercial field-service SLAs are actually written, and it is what makes dispatch delay and travel time SLA-relevant in the first place.

East Valley97.9%93.2%4.7 pts

East Valley's overall SLA rate (97.9%) looks unremarkable — it would not trip an aggregate-level alert, and would even sit within a point of the field. The gap only becomes visible when the analysis is segmented to the tight-window (1-hour) urgent-response jobs, where East Valley trails every other region and posts the widest overall-to-tight SLA drop-off in the dataset. The underlying driver is geography: East Valley's average technician travel time is roughly triple Central District's, and this project's generator deliberately encoded that difference to make exactly this kind of aggregate-vs-segment discrepancy visible — a reminder that a single blended SLA number is not sufficient evidence of dispatch health.

08 · Data quality: what was checked, and what it caught

Three anomaly types were deliberately injected into the synthetic dataset (~0.45–0.5% of completed jobs each) so the data-quality layer would have real, known-quantity issues to detect rather than a clean dataset with nothing to validate against:

CheckRateStatus
Timestamp inversion0.452%PASS
Missing actuals0.452%PASS
Extreme duration0.452%PASS
Orphan dimension keys0.000%Clean
Inconsistent completion flags0.000%Clean

Excel/SQL cross-check: exact match on all three non-zero anomaly types (169/169 each) — two independently-written implementations of the same check, in two different tools, agreeing exactly.

A real bug this cross-checking caught. While validating the Priority Action Queue, the Excel formula layer and the SQL view disagreed on First-Time-Fix/SLA/Callback percentages for specific technicians — not by rounding, but by tens of percentage points. Root cause: vw_TechnicianKPIMonthly joined a technician's jobs without a date filter, so a technician's monthly quality metrics were silently averaged across their entire 2-year history rather than the selected month. Region-level KPIs were unaffected. The original UAT suite didn't catch it because its test fixture only ever had one month of data — nothing to leak across. The fix and a new regression test (UAT-06a/06b) are in sql/04_kpi_views.sql and sql/06_uat_test_cases.sql; the full account is in docs/data_validation_report.md §5. This is the practical argument for building the same KPI logic twice, independently, rather than having Excel simply display SQL's output.

09 · Management recommendations

Dispatch Manager · This week

Rebalance South Metro against North Metro before adding headcount anywhere

South Metro's structural underutilization (averaging 47.2%, and never overlapping any other region's range across 24 months) should be addressed by demand-shifting or reallocating routing before authorizing any new hires in higher-intensity regions — the capacity gap is a distribution problem first, a headcount problem only if rebalancing doesn't close it.

Regional Ops Lead, East Valley · This month

Review dispatch routing/travel radius specifically for 1-hour-SLA job types

Because the gap doesn't show up in East Valley's overall SLA number, this requires deliberately segmenting the review to urgent-response jobs — a blended monthly SLA report would keep missing it.

Training Lead · Next quarter

Pair Apprentice technicians with Master technicians on a rotating basis

The ~4.7x callback-rate gap between Apprentice and Master technicians is large and consistent enough to justify a structured pairing/mentorship program rather than one-off coaching — target the specific technicians flagged in the priority queue first.

Analytics/BI Owner · Ongoing

Keep the SQL and Excel KPI logic independent, and re-run the UAT suite after any view change

Section 08's bug was caught specifically because two independent implementations existed to disagree with each other. Collapsing them into one (e.g., Excel importing SQL's pre-aggregated output) would remove that safety net.

10 · Appendix — traceability, UAT, reproducibility

Requirements traceability: 10 business requirements (BR-01–BR-10), each mapped to a KPI/field, its SQL/Excel implementation, and a specific validation test — full matrix in docs/requirements_traceability_matrix.md, mirrored in the Excel workbook's Traceability_UAT sheet.

UAT suite: 8 test cases (sql/06_uat_test_cases.sql) run inside a transaction against a fully isolated fixture and roll back automatically — the production dataset is never modified by running it. All 8 currently PASS, including a regression test (UAT-06a/06b) added specifically to close the gap that let the Section 08 bug through.

Reproducibility: the entire pipeline — schema, synthetic data generator, data-quality views, KPI views, stored procedures, UAT suite, Excel workbook builder, and this dashboard's data exports — runs from the SQL scripts and the excel/Build_Workbook.ps1 script in this repository.
